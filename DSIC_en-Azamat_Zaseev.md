## Abstract

We propose a kinematic model in which the Universe has no absolute scale. Space and objectness (everything that exists in space) are treated as two poles of one conserved norm, $S^2+O^2=R^2$, and only their ratios are observable. If the objectness fraction $\mu=|O|/R$ changes uniformly and the observer’s rod and clock are made of objectness, the observable scale factor is $b(\mu)=\sqrt{1-\mu^2}/\mu^2$, and the whole late history of expansion follows from a single law, $b^2(\tau)=e^{4k\tau}-e^{2k\tau}$. The law has one shape parameter, the present phase $\mu_0$, as many as flat ΛCDM. In this picture acceleration is the divergence of the two scale factors seen from inside; it begins at $\mu^2=3-\sqrt5$. On the Pantheon+SH0ES supernovae the model is statistically indistinguishable from flat ΛCDM: $\Delta\chi^2=-0.1$ at $\mu_0=0.760\pm0.009$, which corresponds to the onset of acceleration at $z_t=0.77\pm0.07$.

This geometry alone cannot describe the early Universe: its particle horizon lies closer than the last-scattering surface, and its expansion rate at nucleosynthesis is 24 times the standard one. A second principle is therefore introduced: matter is contained in space and cannot go beyond it, so when the scale of space is small, matter is compressed as in a press. Since the size of an object is held by its own centre, compressed objectness behaves as dust, $w=0$. With no fitted numbers, this yields the switch-on point of the core, $\kappa^2=4+2\sqrt7$ (which is also the point $q=1/2$), the law by which internal time accelerates, and one expansion-rate formula for the whole history, resting on both principles. The only external input to the formula itself is the radiation density, known from the temperature of the cosmic microwave background (CMB). The sound horizon also uses the measured density of atoms, and $H_0$ is found from the measured CMB angle. The early history has the standard form, matter and radiation with the same rate at nucleosynthesis, while the amount of matter is set by the core. At $\mu_0=0.76$ the model gives a sound horizon $r_*=142.7$ Mpc, $H_0=67.9$ km/s/Mpc from the CMB angle, and an age of 13.69 Gyr.

The model describes the full Planck 2018 CMB spectrum (the compressed plik-lite likelihood) no worse than ΛCDM: $\Delta\chi^2=-2.5$ with the same number of parameters. In a joint fit with supernovae and the thirteen DESI DR2 BAO points it loses: $\Delta\chi^2=+5.4$ with the CMB angle alone and $+8.8$ with the full spectrum. The loss has one cause: at the phase preferred by the late-time data, the model’s own sound horizon is 1.8 % shorter than BAO require. The discrepancy sits between $z\approx2$ and recombination, where matter drives the expansion, and not near the zero of space. If the matter fraction is left free, the full spectrum together with supernovae and BAO gives $f=0.944\pm0.014$, that is, about 6 % less matter than the core sets, and $\Delta\chi^2=-7.7$ relative to ΛCDM ($-5.7$ with the Akaike correction for the extra parameter, a tie by the Bayesian criterion); the phases preferred by the late-time data and by the CMB then agree. The model does not yet derive this fraction. It does not explain the nature of non-luminous matter (about 85 % of all matter), it has no perturbation theory of its own, and its rule for measuring scale remains a postulate. The paper keeps what is derived apart from what is postulated and lists the observations that would refute the model.

Keywords: cosmology, accelerated expansion, dark energy, relativity of scale, type Ia supernovae, baryon acoustic oscillations, cosmic microwave background.

## In plain words

### The main idea

Everything has a boundary. An atom, a stone, a star, a galaxy: each is a local bulge, with a centre, an inside and an edge, and each of them is somewhere. All of this together I call objectness.

Space is arranged the other way round. It has no edge and no outer boundary. It is concave on all sides at once. Space itself is nowhere, because the word “where” has meaning only inside it.

My idea is that objectness and space are two sides of one quantity, scale. The total stock of scale is conserved, so one side can increase its scale factor only at the expense of the other. Neither objectness nor space has an absolute size: there is only their ratio to each other.

Right now things are losing scale by falling into themselves, and space is gaining it. Everything falls: galaxies, stars, atoms, and the particles that atoms are made of.

### Why we do not notice it

We ourselves are made of objectness. Our rulers and clocks change along with us, so we do not see our own contraction. What we see is something else: the distances between distant galaxies grow, and they grow ever faster.

The scale flow can be pictured like this. You measure a room with a ruler, and the ruler slowly shortens while the room grows. By your measurements the room grows faster and faster, although nothing pushes it. This is how DSIC explains the accelerated expansion of the Universe: it is not the work of “dark energy” but the divergence of two scales, seen from inside.

The stronger the gravitational binding between objects, galaxies and clusters of galaxies, the more pronounced their common centre of fall, and the distances between them do not grow. New space arrives where gravitational binding is weakest: in the voids between clusters.

### What there was at the beginning

The Big Bang is often pictured like this: all matter is squeezed into a point in the middle of a vast empty space, then the point explodes, and the fragments fly off in all directions. The image is tenacious, but it is wrong: standard cosmology does not describe the beginning this way either. DSIC says what there was instead.

There was no vast space. The scale factor of space was at its minimum, and that of objectness at its maximum. Matter was not squeezed into a point: it filled the whole of space, only there was very little space. Kilometres of matter were compressed into a millimetre of space.

Matter cannot go beyond space, so space pressed it into a continuous monolith. What state matter was in at the very minimum of space, I do not know, and the model does not say.

What is called an explosion is not a scattering from one point. It is the break-up of the monolith at all of its points at once.

Take a cubic metre of matter and mentally cut it into small cubes. Let every cube begin to shrink, losing scale, while what it loses goes to the gaps between the cubes. For any cube all the others recede, and the farther the neighbour, the faster. There is no centre of the expansion: from whichever cube you look, the picture is the same.

In the model the fall is real: it sets the entire dynamics. But the ruler is made of the same stuff that falls, and there is no external ruler, so only the ratio of the two scale factors can be measured. Whether the cubes shrink or the gaps grow, from inside it is one event seen from two sides, and no measurement can tell the two descriptions apart.

### Two times and the core of the model

The scale flow itself, which I call the core, has its own even time. From inside it cannot be seen. Inside space, time is relative: our clocks are made of objectness and run the faster, the smaller its scale.

The core can be pictured as a circle with a point moving along its edge. The position of the point sets both quantities at once. The height of the point above the centre of the circle is the scale factor of objectness. The sideways offset of the point from the centre is the scale factor of space. The higher the point, the more objectness and the less space, and the other way round. What changes uniformly is not the path of the point along the arc but its height, that is, the scale factor of objectness.

- **The point at the very top.** Objectness is at its maximum, space at zero. This is the compressed monolith, the start of the cycle.
- **The point moves down along the arc.** Objectness decreases, space grows. This is our epoch: by now the point has moved about 40° from the top.
- **The point at the side, level with the centre.** Objectness is zero, space is at its maximum.
- **The point below the centre.** Objectness grows again, but now in the mirror phase, and space contracts.
- **The point at the very bottom.** Space is at zero again: things have merged into a new monolith.

Objectness decreases to zero, passes through it and begins to grow in the mirror phase. Space meanwhile starts to contract again, until things collide and merge into a new monolith. Then space contracts so strongly that it passes through its own zero, everting everything it contains, and it all begins anew. This is how our cycle began, too.

From inside it looks different. The closer the zero of objectness, the faster our clocks run, and there is always infinitely much of our time left before it. An inner observer will never see objectness pass through its zero: for them the Universe simply expands faster and faster.

### What the test showed

This is not just an image. A formula for the expansion of the Universe follows from these principles, and it can be compared with measurements.

- On distant supernovae the model describes the expansion as well as the standard one does.
- It describes the cosmic microwave background no worse.
- When all the data are put together, the standard model is ahead, by about three standard deviations. This is close to the threshold beyond which the model in its present form should be considered rejected.
- About 85 % of matter must be non-luminous. What it is, the model does not explain, and neither does the standard one.

## 1. Introduction

In 1998 two teams studying distant supernovae found that the expansion of the Universe is accelerating [1, 2]. The standard ΛCDM model describes this with the cosmological constant Λ, a component whose density does not drop as the Universe expands. The description works very well, but Λ itself remains a number written into the equations, not derived. In 2025 the DESI data, together with the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and supernovae, gave a noticeable hint that “dark energy” may change with time [3]. The question of its nature has become sharper.

A different question is asked here. Can the same expansion curve arise with no energy ingredient at all, from what scale is and how it is measured by an observer who is part of the Universe?

The idea is simple. If you measure a room with a ruler that slowly shortens while the room grows, then by your measurements the room grows faster and faster, although nothing pushes it. In DSIC the ruler is made of one pole of scale (objectness), and the room of the other (space). They are tied by one conserved norm, so the growth of one means the loss of the other. The observed acceleration turns out to be the divergence of these two scale factors.

The paper shows how far this idea goes under strict calculation.

- The late Universe follows from a single function $b(\mu)$ and describes supernovae, BAO and cosmic chronometers on a par with flat ΛCDM, with the same number of parameters (§2–§7).
- This function alone does not describe the early Universe, and the reason is quantitative (§8). A second principle is needed: matter is contained in space and, when the scale of space is small, it is compressed as in a press. Together with the core, it gives the law of internal time and one rate formula for the whole history (§9); that compressed matter behaves as dust remains an assumption.
- In joint fits with supernovae, BAO and the CMB the model loses to ΛCDM: by 2.3σ with the CMB angle alone and by 3.0σ with the full spectrum (§10). The cause is one: the model’s own sound horizon is 1.8 % shorter than BAO require (§10.2). If there is about 6 % less matter than the switch-on point gives, the model describes all three data sets no worse than ΛCDM (§10.3); deriving this fraction from the principles has not yet been achieved. These results are reported on an equal footing with the rest.

Every step carries a status, so that it is clear what it rests on: *(postulate)* is an adopted rule; *(derived)* follows from the postulates mathematically; *(tested)* is confirmed by calculation on data; *(open)* is unresolved; *(against the model)* is a result that works against it. ## 2. Principles

The computational part of the model is built from six principles. Two rules are added to them, which are likewise adopted rather than derived: the rule for measuring scale (§4) and the rate of internal time in compressed matter (§9.3). The first five principles together with the measurement rule give the late Universe; the sixth and the internal-time rule are needed for the early one. The qualitative picture of objects and space on which they rest is set out in §2.1.

**P1. No absolute scale.** Only the ratio of scales has physical meaning. No construction in the model may give access to an “absolute” length.

**P2. Two poles, one norm.** Space and objectness are two poles of one relation. An object is a local bulge: it has an inside and a boundary. Space is a universal concavity: it is nowhere itself, and the category “where” arises only inside it. The scale factors of the poles, $S$ and $O$, obey one conservation law:

$$
S^2+O^2=R^2=\mathrm{const}. \tag{1}
$$

One pole can grow only at the expense of the other. Everywhere below, the phrase “scale flows” means exactly this: the scale factor of objectness changes relative to the scale factor of space, and vice versa. By P1, nothing absolute is carried over.

**P3. The objectness fraction flows uniformly.** The system has one degree of freedom, the objectness fraction $\mu=|O|/R$. In core time $t$ it changes at a constant rate:

$$
\left|\frac{d\mu}{dt}\right|=k,\qquad k>0. \tag{2}
$$

On our branch of the cycle the objectness fraction decreases, $d\mu/dt=-k$; in the mirror phase its modulus grows at the same rate (§3). Core time is the time of the scale flow itself; it cannot be observed from inside. What flows uniformly is $\mu$, the quantity in which all objects are measured, and not the angle on the circle (3). The alternative with a uniform angle is rejected: it does not give the deceleration that took place in the past (§5.1).

**P4. The observer is inside.** Every observer is an object inside the system, and its instruments change along with it. The signs of quantities and absolute scale are out of its reach; it has access only to the moduli $P=|S|$, $Q=|O|$ and their ratios.

**P5. The speed of light is constant in the observer’s units.** The observer’s rod and clock are linked through $c=\mathrm{const}$. This is not an absolute quantity but one more ratio: the exchange rate of length for time.

**P6. Matter does not go beyond space.** Matter is contained in space, and it has nowhere to go beyond it: space has no outer side (§2.1). Therefore, when the scale of space is small, matter is compressed as in a press, while the amount of objectness in each comoving volume stays the same. Under compression its density grows exactly as many times as the volume shrinks: if all sizes are halved, the density grows eightfold. Only the density of light grows faster: compression also shortens each of its waves, and under the same conditions it grows sixteenfold.

In physics this difference is written with the equation of state $w$: for dust $w=0$, for light $w=1/3$. That compressed objectness behaves precisely as dust does not follow from P6 alone but from P6 together with the picture of §2.1: the size of an object is held by its own centre, so the press compresses the medium, not the objects themselves, and adds no pressure to them. In the calculations this is written as $w=0$ for compressed objectness; an attempt to test this assumption on data is discussed in §10.1. The principle does not change the late Universe and acts only where the compression is large (§9).

### 2.1 Objects, the concavity of space, and connectedness

The postulates say how the scale factors are arranged. Here we describe what the objects themselves and the space between them look like in this picture. This is the qualitative part of the model: the calculations below use only its homogeneous average. *(postulate)*

**Objectness.** Everything that is inside space is objectness: matter, light, and what does not shine. An object is a form of it that has an inside and a boundary. Rods, clocks and the observer are objects too. An object is a local bulge of scale: its boundary faces outward, toward space. The quantity $O$ is the scale factor of all objects at once, and they share one fraction $\mu$ (P3).

**Space is concave on all sides.** The bulge of an object and the concavity of space are one and the same boundary seen from two sides. Wherever an object is, space faces it with its inner side and encloses it entirely. Space has no outer side and no edge, so it is nowhere itself. The concavity is the same in all directions, and space has no preferred direction.

**Objects fall into themselves.** When objectness gives up scale to space, every object contracts toward its own centre rather than moving along some common vector. There is no point toward which the whole Universe would fall. The fall goes on at all levels at once: clusters, galaxies, stars, atoms, atomic nuclei, and what nuclei are made of, quarks and gluons, all fall into themselves. From inside the fall cannot be seen: rods and clocks contract together with the object. The observer sees something else: the growth of space between objects, the same in all directions from any place.

**What has a centre.** A centre belongs to a bound whole that can be at rest: a particle with mass, an atom, a star. Everything locked inside such a whole falls into its centre together with it, even if it has no rest mass of its own. This is how the proton is built: about 99 % of its mass is the energy of the gluon field and of the motion of the quarks locked inside. Free light is not locked in any object. It has no rest and no centre, and the length of its wave is set by space. On this difference rests the fact that compressed objectness behaves as dust, while light does not (P6).

**But not always.** This is how objects behave in our phase of the cycle (§3). When objectness passes its zero, objects will start to grow in the mirror phase, and space will contract again. In the end the objects will collide and find themselves compressed by space. Then space itself will pass through its zero and undergo eversion: all its coordinates will become mirrored, and the objectness inside will be forced out of the mirror phase and start falling into itself again. This is how the next cycle begins, and this is how ours began: the compressed early Universe is objectness that has just been brought out of the mirror phase. That is why in our phase it falls into itself from the very start, even in its most compressed state.

**Binding and the common centre.** Objects bound by gravity do not fall separately but together. The stronger the binding, the more pronounced their common centre of fall. A strongly bound system contracts as one whole, and the distances inside it, measured with its own rods, do not grow. This is why a planetary system, a galaxy and a cluster do not expand. For weakly bound objects the common centre is blurred; unbound ones have none.

**Where space arrives.** New space arrives where gravitational connectedness is lowest: in the voids between clusters and filaments. So the expansion is inhomogeneous: inside bound systems there is none, and in the voids it is above average. This agrees with observations: the Local Group of galaxies is separated from the Hubble flow by a zero-velocity radius of $0.96\pm0.03$ Mpc [4]. But this fact does not distinguish DSIC from ΛCDM: there, too, bound systems do not expand. A related idea, that expansion and the different rates of clocks are tied to voids, is developed by the timescape model [5].

**Consistency with P3.** Only the core has a single number $\mu$ and a common time. Inside space everything is local: where exactly space arrives is decided by local gravity, and time runs differently in different places. It is assumed that, averaged over volume, the expansion equals that of the core.

**Two meanings of a word.** Below we distinguish the *scale of objectness*, the pole $O$ and its fraction $\mu$, which decrease in our phase, from the *amount of objectness* in a comoving volume, which by P6 is conserved (§9.3). The first describes the size of the observer’s rod, the second how much matter is locked in the volume.

There is no quantitative theory of the common centre yet. All calculations below use a homogeneous background, that is, the volume average, as ΛCDM does; structure growth is computed with the equations of general relativity. *(open)*

## 3. The core and the full cycle

Both poles can be conveniently written through one angle $\theta$ on a circle of radius $R$, or as one complex number:

$$
S=R\sin\theta,\qquad O=R\cos\theta,\qquad \psi=O+iS=R\,e^{i\theta}. \tag{3}
$$

The norm $|\psi|^2=R^2$ is conserved identically. The state of the system is a point on the circle; it cannot move inward or outward. The angle $\theta$ is only a convenient coordinate. By P3 it is $\mu=|\cos\theta|$ that flows uniformly, so the angular velocity of the point varies:

$$
\left|\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right|=\frac{k}{|\sin\theta|}. \tag{4}
$$

At the zero of objectness ($\theta=90^\circ$) it is minimal and equal to $k$. At the zero of space ($\theta=0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$) it grows without bound: near it $S\approx R\sqrt{2k|t|}$, and $\mu(t)$ has a kink. The zero of space is thus a singular point of the motion of the core, not a smooth passage. *(derived)*

In one cycle, from one zero of space to the next, the point travels half the circle, and four events take place along the way.

**Stage 1, $\theta$ from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$.** Space gains scale and objectness loses it. The compressed medium expands, and objects separate from one another and fall into themselves. This is the branch we live on.

**Stage 2, the zero of objectness, $\theta=90^\circ$.** Objectness passes through zero and changes sign. Space is at its maximum at this moment and does not change sign. An inner observer sees only the modulus: for them objectness touches zero and turns back. No force is needed for the turn: at the level of the core there is one smooth forward motion, and the turn is produced by taking the modulus.

**Stage 3, $\theta$ from $90^\circ$ to $180^\circ$.** On the mirror sheet of objectness its modulus grows, while space contracts toward zero. Objects grow, collide and merge into a continuous mass, which space squeezes ever harder.

**Stage 4, the zero of space, $\theta=180^\circ$.** Space itself passes through zero and undergoes eversion: all its coordinates become mirrored. Space is the common container, so together with it everything inside changes sign, the observer’s instruments included. Objectness that was in the mirror phase is forced out of it and starts falling into itself again. The sign of objectness inherited from the container is written without separate rules:

$$
O_{\rm full}=R\cos\theta\cdot\operatorname{sgn}(\sin\theta). \tag{5}
$$

The quantity $O_{\rm full}$ is continuous at the zero of objectness and changes sign abruptly at the zero of space. From inside the jump cannot be observed: the system and the instruments change sign at the same time. The next cycle runs on the mirror sheet of space, and all signs return to where they started only after two observed cycles: $\psi(\theta+\pi)=-\psi(\theta)$, $\psi(\theta+2\pi)=\psi(\theta)$.

**The compressed phase (“mush”).** This is the name not of a moment but of a stretch of the cycle on both sides of the zero of space. The scale of objectness there is large and that of space small: objects are merged into a continuous mass. Before the zero of space (the end of stage 3) the mass is being compressed; after the zero (the start of stage 1) it expands. Very close to the zero its state is unknown. The compressed phase does not coincide with the equality of the poles: the equality $|S|=|O|$ occurs at $\theta=45^\circ$ and $135^\circ$, far from it. The model sets no sharp boundary for it. *(postulate)*

> **A caveat about signs**
>
> All the sign mechanics belongs to the ontology of the model and is not a source of numbers. The observer sees only $\mu$, so none of the formulas below depends on signs. The two-sheet structure is forced by the fact that the modulus identifies $\psi$ with $-\psi$; it yields no new observables.

## 4. The view from inside: rod, clock, scale

By P4 the observer sees the moduli:

$$
P=|S|=R\sqrt{1-\mu^2},\qquad Q=|O|=R\mu,\qquad \mu\in[0,1]. \tag{6}
$$

**The rod.** The observer is made of objectness, so its unit of length is proportional to the objectness pole: $L\propto\mu$. Space measured with such a rod is the ratio $P/Q=\sqrt{1-\mu^2}/\mu$. *(derived from P2, P4)*

**The clock.** Suppose light covers the same distance at core scale in each interval of core time $dt$, and the period of the observer’s clock is $T\propto\mu^{p}$. Then the speed of light in the observer’s units is $c\propto T/L\propto\mu^{p-1}$. P5 requires it to be constant, which is possible only for $p=1$:

$$
d\tau=\frac{dt}{\mu}. \tag{7}
$$

Here $\tau$ is the observer’s proper time, the only time that can be measured from inside. The smaller the scale of objectness, the more ticks of the clock fit into a unit of core time. *(derived from P5)*

**The scale.** The observable scale factor is defined as space measured with a rod made of objectness and converted by a clock made of objectness:

$$
b(\mu)=\frac{P}{Q}\cdot\frac{1}{\mu}=\frac{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}{\mu^2}. \tag{8}
$$

This is the key measurement rule of the model. It is not derived but adopted. *(postulate)* The factor $1/\mu$ is the rate of the internal clock (7); core time $t$ does not enter $b$.

The status of this factor must be stated plainly. If only a wavelength is measured with the rod, or only a period with the clock, one gets a single factor $\mu$, that is, $b=P/Q$ and $q=2\mu^2-1$. The second factor does not follow from postulates P1–P5. The data support it: without it the supernovae are described just as well, but together with the shape of DESI BAO the fit is worse by $\Delta\chi^2=9.1$ with the same number of parameters (§7). *(tested)* Why the clock rate enters the scale remains an open question (§14).

**Redshift.** Light travels through space but is measured with instruments made of objectness. The ratio of wavelengths includes the stretching of space (the pole $P$), the ratio of the rods of the source and the observer, and the ratio of their clocks: $(P_0/P_e)\cdot(\mu_e/\mu_0)\cdot(\mu_e/\mu_0)$. As a result, the observed wavelength is proportional to $b$:

$$
1+z=\frac{b(\mu_0)}{b(\mu_e)}=\frac{\sqrt{1-\mu_0^2}}{\sqrt{1-\mu_e^2}}\cdot\frac{\mu_e^2}{\mu_0^2}, \tag{9}
$$

where $\mu_0$ is the present phase and $\mu_e$ the phase at the moment of emission. The first factor is the contribution of space, the second that of the objectness scale. The function $b$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,1)$, so each $z$ corresponds to exactly one $\mu_e$. The inverse relation is written explicitly: with $B=b_0/(1+z)$ and $s=\sqrt{1+4B^2}$,

$$
\mu_e^2=\frac{2}{1+s},\qquad \frac{P_e}{R}=\frac{2B}{1+s}. \tag{10}
$$

The balance of the contributions shows in numbers. At recombination, $1+z=1091$ is made up of a 630-fold growth of space and a $1/\mu_0^2=1.73$-fold decrease of the objectness scale (at $\mu_0=0.76$). Since the start of the cycle, objects have lost 24 % of their scale. In the past the expansion came almost entirely from the growth of space; in the future it will come from the decrease of the objectness scale.

## 5. Kinematics: where acceleration comes from

From P3 and the clock (7), on our branch $d\mu/d\tau=\mu\,d\mu/dt=-k\mu$. Counting $\tau$ from the zero of space, where $\mu=1$, gives the law by which the observer sees the scale flow:

$$
\mu(\tau)=e^{-k\tau}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b^2(\tau)=e^{4k\tau}-e^{2k\tau}. \tag{11}
$$

The whole history of the core fits into one line. *(derived)* At small $\tau$ the scale grows as $\sqrt{2k\tau}$, as under radiation domination; at large $\tau$ as $e^{2k\tau}$, which is de Sitter expansion.

The expansion rate and the deceleration parameter follow by differentiation:

$$
\begin{aligned}H&=\frac{1}{b}\frac{db}{d\tau}=k\,\frac{2-\mu^2}{1-\mu^2},\\[4pt] q&=-1-\frac{1}{H^2}\frac{dH}{d\tau}=\frac{-\mu^4+6\mu^2-4}{(2-\mu^2)^2}.\end{aligned} \tag{12}
$$

The rate can also be written through the scale itself, without $\mu$:

$$
H=k\,\frac{s\,(s+1)}{2b^2},\qquad s=\sqrt{1+4b^2}. \tag{13}
$$

The present value links the dimensionless geometry to units of km/s/Mpc:

$$
H_0=kK,\qquad K=\frac{2-\mu_0^2}{1-\mu_0^2}. \tag{14}
$$

The parameter $q$ depends only on the phase and changes sign at exactly one point:

$$
\begin{aligned}&q=0\iff\mu^2=3-\sqrt5,\\[2pt] &\mu_\times=0.874032,\qquad b(\mu_\times)=0.636010.\end{aligned} \tag{15}
$$

There is also a second special point, needed in §9. There $q=1/2$, that is, the effective equation of state $w=(2q-1)/3$ is zero, as for free matter:

$$
\begin{aligned}&q=\tfrac12\iff\mu_h^2=\frac{8-2\sqrt7}{3},\\[2pt] &\mu_h=0.950175,\qquad b(\mu_h)=0.345266.\end{aligned} \tag{16}
$$

The values of $\mu$ at the special points are pure numbers of the model. The redshifts of these points depend on the present phase: $1+z_t=b_0/b(\mu_\times)$ and $1+z_h=b_0/b(\mu_h)$, where $b_0=b(\mu_0)$ (Table 1).

The mechanism of acceleration can be read off the decomposition (9). Near the zero of space the factor of space dominates, and the expansion decelerates: $q\to+1$. ### 5.1 Testing P3: why $\mu$ flows and not the angle

The circle sets the relation between the poles but does not say what flows uniformly. If the angle $\theta$ advanced uniformly, then $d\mu/dt\propto-\sqrt{1-\mu^2}$, and the same calculation would give

$$
q_\theta=-1+\frac{\mu^4}{(2-\mu^2)^2}\le 0. \tag{17}
$$

Such a Universe would never have decelerated, while deceleration in the past is reliably measured ($z_t=0.74\pm0.05$ [6]). On supernovae this variant loses to ΛCDM by $\Delta\chi^2=+3.0$. This is why P3 states the uniform flow precisely for the fraction $\mu$. *(tested)*

### 5.2 Proper time and the two zeros

From (11), the proper time elapsed from the zero of space to the phase $\mu$ is $\tau=-\ln\mu/k$, and the core time is $t=(1-\mu)/k$. This implies an asymmetry between the two zeros that should be named plainly. *(derived)*

- **The zero of objectness is unreachable from inside.** A finite core time, $\mu_0/k$, remains until $\mu=0$, but an infinite proper time: the observer’s clock runs ever faster and manages infinitely many ticks. At $\mu_0=0.76$ and $H_0=67.9$ km/s/Mpc one gets $1/k=48.5$ Gyr, and $\mu$ halves every 33.6 Gyr of proper time. For an inner observer this is eternal accelerating expansion.
- **The mirror phase begins in the infinite past.** By the same formula, for an observer in stage 3 the zero of objectness lies in the infinitely distant proper past. The infinite future of one phase joins the infinite past of the other only in core time.
- **The zero of space lies at a finite distance in time.** A finite proper time has elapsed from it to the present day (the age of the Universe, §9). But the observer does not witness the transition itself for a different reason: in the compressed phase there are no separate objects, and the state of matter at the very zero is unknown.

The equality of the poles, $\mu=1/\sqrt2$, is still ahead: with the same parameters it is 3.5 Gyr away.

## 6. Distances, horizon and velocities

Light travels at speed $c$ in the local units of each epoch. The path $c\,d\tau$ covered in $d\tau$ equals $c\,d\tau\cdot b_0/b$ in present (comoving) units. With $d\tau=-d\mu/(k\mu)$ the integral can be taken in closed form:

$$
\chi(\mu_e)=\frac{c}{k}\,b_0\left[\sqrt{1-\mu_0^2}-\sqrt{1-\mu_e^2}\,\right],\qquad \frac{c}{k}=\frac{c}{H_0}\,K. \tag{18}
$$

From this follow the luminosity distance $d_L=(1+z)\chi$ and the distance modulus. The identity $d\chi/dz=c/H$ holds exactly; a numerical check agrees to machine rounding. *(derived)*

As $\mu_e\to1$ the distance stays finite. In the geometry of the core a particle horizon exists in its own right:

$$
\chi_{\rm hor}=\frac{c}{k}\,\frac{1-\mu_0^2}{\mu_0^2}, \tag{19}
$$

which at $\mu_0=0.76$ and $H_0=67.4$ km/s/Mpc (the Planck value in ΛCDM: the core alone has no $H_0$ of its own from the CMB, since for it the CMB lies beyond the horizon) amounts to 10954 Mpc. This matters for §8: in the geometry of the core alone, light cannot arrive from anything beyond this distance.

**Velocities.** Objects at a comoving distance $d$ recede at the speed $v=H\,(b/b_0)\,d$. Beyond the Hubble sphere this speed exceeds the speed of light. There is no contradiction: this is not motion through space but the growth of space itself between objects. In the epoch of acceleration the Hubble sphere shrinks in comoving coordinates, and objects one after another lose causal contact with us.

> **Why there is no Lorentz contraction here, and one coincidence**
>
> The peculiar velocities of galaxies are hundreds of kilometres per second, and the Lorentz correction $\gamma-1$ is of order $10^{-6}$. Recession faster than light is the growth of space between galaxies, not their flight through space, and Lorentz contraction does not apply to it.
>
> The coincidence lies elsewhere. The circle of moduli (6) is written in the same way as the relation between velocity and the Lorentz factor:
>
> $$
> \mu^2+\left(\frac{P}{R}\right)^2=1\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{\gamma^2}+\frac{v^2}{c^2}=1.
> $$
>
> If the space fraction $P/R$ is taken as a “velocity” $v/c$, then the objectness fraction $\mu$ is $1/\gamma$, and the observer’s rod $L\propto\mu$ shortens exactly as Lorentz contraction requires. Today this would be “as if” $v=0.65c$ and $\gamma=1.32$.
>
> The coincidence breaks down for clocks. In relativity moving clocks run slower, $d\tau=\mu\,dt$; in DSIC the observer’s clocks run faster, $d\tau=dt/\mu$. With “Lorentzian” clocks the measured speed of light would change as $1/\mu^2$: at $z=1.15$ ($\mu=0.904$) it would be 29 % lower than today, and the fine-structure constant 41 % higher. Quasar spectra at this $z$ give $\Delta\alpha/\alpha=(1.3\pm1.4)\cdot10^{-6}$ [7], hundreds of thousands of times smaller. Such a variant is ruled out. DSIC is a consistent scaling of rod and clock under which $c$ does not change. The coincidence of the circle with the Lorentz formula is noted as an observation; the model offers no explanation for it. *(open)*

## 8. Where the core ends

The core describes the late Universe. If it is continued all the way to the zero of space, two problems arise, and both are quantitative.

**The CMB lies beyond the horizon.** The angle of the acoustic scale on the CMB, $\theta_*$ [14], and BAO at $z=2.33$ together fix a ratio of distances almost without model assumptions. All that is needed is that the sound horizon at recombination, $r_*$, and at the baryon drag epoch, $r_d$, relate as in all known models, $r_*/r_d\approx0.98$:

$$
\frac{D_M(z_*)}{D_M(2.33)}=\frac{r_*/r_d}{\theta_*\,[D_M/r_d]_{2.33}}\approx2.42. \tag{20}
$$

In the core, light cannot arrive from beyond its own horizon (19):

$$
\frac{D_M(z_*)}{D_M(2.33)}\lt\frac{\chi_{\rm hor}}{D_M(2.33)}=1.89. \tag{21}
$$

No choice of clock can fix this as long as light follows the geometry of the core: the path from the zero of space to us is finite and shorter than required. Nor can the path of light or the rate of clocks be corrected separately: in any metric of the form $ds^2=-c^2d\tau^2+b^2dx^2$, distance, time and rate are all computed from one function $H(z)$. It is $H(z)$ itself that must be corrected. *(derived)*

**Too fast an early expansion.** Near the zero of space the core behaves as radiation. From (13) as $b\to0$, for $E_{\rm core}=H_{\rm core}/H_0$ one gets

$$
E_{\rm core}(z)\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{(1+z)^2}{K\,b_0^2}=0.2345\,(1+z)^2, \tag{22}
$$

where the number is given for $\mu_0=0.76$. In density this is about 600 times the real radiation (photons and neutrinos). At nucleosynthesis ($z\sim4\cdot10^8$) the rate would be 24 times the standard one, and far more helium would form than the observed 24–25 %.

Both problems are one symptom: at $z\gtrsim3$ the core expands too fast.

## 9. Compression and internal time

### 9.1 Degree of compression

P6 says that matter cannot go beyond space. How much objectness there is per unit of space is shown by the ratio of the poles

$$
\kappa(\mu)=\frac{Q}{P}=\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}. \tag{23}
$$

At $\mu_0=0.76$, today $\kappa=1.17$, at $z=10$ about 10, and at recombination about 970: the scale factor of objectness is almost a thousand times that of space.

$Q$ and $P$ cannot be compared head-on. The boundary $Q=P$ falls at $\mu=1/\sqrt2$, that is, at $z=-0.20$, in the future: by such a rule even today’s matter would be compressed. Hence compression according to P6 begins to affect the rate only from some threshold $\kappa_h$. Below we show that the threshold is not fitted but follows from the core.

### 9.2 What compression does not do

The most straightforward option is to assume that space additionally compresses the objects themselves, rods and clocks included. In the rule (8) this makes the scale $b$ non-monotonic: light from the compressed phase stops being redshifted beyond the threshold, and a CMB at $z=1090$ becomes impossible. Partial compression shortens both the path and the time even more than the core does. There is also a direct observation against it: hydrogen recombined at a temperature of about 3000 K, as in the laboratory, which means that in their own units atoms at $z=1090$ were the same as today.

Conclusion: the pressure of space does not change the size of objects in the observer’s units. What is compressed is the medium, that is, the gaps between objects. This does not cancel the falling into themselves: at core scale all objects, from clusters to quarks, shrink together with $\mu$, both in the compressed phase and today; it simply cannot be seen from inside. Compression changes something else: the rate of internal time of compressed matter.

### 9.3 Internal time of compressed matter

Only the core has core time. Inside space every kind of matter has its own time. For free objectness this is the clock (7). For compressed matter an acceleration factor $\Sigma\ge1$ is introduced:

$$
d\tau=\Sigma(\kappa)\,\frac{dt}{\mu}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H=\frac{H_{\rm core}}{\Sigma}. \tag{24}
$$

The subscript “core” refers to the core: $H_{\rm core}$ is the rate that the geometry of the core alone would give (12). If internal time runs faster, more proper time accumulates per unit of scale flow, and light, moving at $c$ in local units, covers more space in that time. What changes is the time per unit of redshift, not the size of things.

This definition must be reconciled with P5. The derivation of (7) assumed that light at core scale covers the same distance per unit of core time. In the compressed phase this assumption is dropped. What stays constant is the speed of light in local units (P5), while the relation between local time and core time changes for everything at once, for clocks and for light alike. Local physics remains that of the laboratory, and only $H(z)$ enters the calculations. *(postulate)*

**Binding mass.** We need a measure of how much objectness there is in a comoving volume. Local gravity provides it. Take a small ball of radius $r=b\,x$ whose edges fly apart at the speed $v=Hr$. For the gravity of its contents to just hold this expansion, the mass of the ball must be $M=v^2r/(2G_N)=H^2r^3/(2G_N)\propto H^2b^3$, where $G_N$ is Newton’s constant. For homogeneous expansion this Newtonian calculation gives the same equation as general relativity does for the whole Universe [15]. The quantity $H^2b^3$ is therefore called the binding mass of a comoving ball. For objectness that falls into itself it is constant: objectness does not leave its volume (P6), and the stretching of space, unlike in the case of light, does not weaken it (§2.1). For light it decreases: as space stretches, each of its waves loses energy. For the divergence of the scales it grows: space is being added. The effective equation of state says the same in one number: the binding mass changes as $b^{-3w}$, so for dust $w=0$, for light $w=1/3$, and for added space $w\lt0$.

For the core the binding mass is expressed exactly through the degree of compression. From (12) and (8), with $\mu^2=\kappa^2/(1+\kappa^2)$,

$$
H_{\rm core}^2\,b^3=k^2\,G(\kappa),\qquad G(\kappa)=\frac{(2+\kappa^2)^2\,(1+\kappa^2)^{3/2}}{\kappa^6}. \tag{25}
$$

$G$ has a single minimum, at the point $\kappa_h$. At weaker compression ($\kappa\lt\kappa_h$, later) the binding mass grows with time: the core adds space, and the effective $w\lt0$. Here P6 changes nothing. At stronger compression ($\kappa\gt\kappa_h$, earlier) the core would make the binding mass decrease with time, that is, objectness would lose it the way light does. P6 does not allow this: objectness has nowhere to go from its volume, and the press adds no pressure to it (§2.1). The binding mass freezes at its minimum value, and the expansion becomes “dust-like”. The rule is written in one line, $w_{\rm obj}=\min(w_{\rm core},0)$. The transition point is not chosen; it is the minimum itself:

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{dG}{d\kappa}=0&\iff\kappa_h^2=4+2\sqrt7\\[2pt] &\iff\mu_h^2=\frac{8-2\sqrt7}{3}\iff q=\tfrac12.\end{aligned} \tag{26}
$$

At this point the binding mass of the core itself is momentarily constant, $w_{\rm core}=0$, so the rate and its first derivative join smoothly. The redshift of the switch-on point $z_h$ depends on $\mu_0$ (Table 1); at $\mu_0=0.76$ it is $z_h=2.26$. *(derived from P6)*

**One rate formula.** Let $E=H/H_0$, and let $\Omega_r$ be the present radiation fraction. The rule $w_{\rm obj}=\min(w_{\rm core},0)$ together with real light gives a rate formula for the whole history. The formula is one, but it rests on two parts of the model: the geometry of the core with the measurement rule (8), and the principle P6:

$$
\begin{aligned}E^2(z)&=(1-\Omega_r)\,(1+z)^3\,F\big(\min(z,z_h)\big)+\Omega_r\,(1+z)^4,\\[4pt] F(z)&=\frac{E_{\rm core}^2(z)}{(1+z)^3}=\frac{G(\kappa(z))}{K^2b_0^3}.\end{aligned} \tag{27}
$$

The radiation density is not fitted: $\Omega_rh^2=4.18\cdot10^{-5}$ follows from the CMB temperature and three generations of neutrinos. The amplitude of the “dust” part follows from continuity at the switch-on point:

$$
\begin{aligned}A_m&=(1-\Omega_r)\,F(z_h)=(1-\Omega_r)\,\frac{G(\kappa_h)}{K^2b_0^3},\\[4pt] G(\kappa_h)&=\frac{(6+2\sqrt7)^2\,(5+2\sqrt7)^{3/2}}{(4+2\sqrt7)^3}=5.2476.\end{aligned} \tag{28}
$$

At $\mu_0=0.76$ this is $A_m=0.3248$. The amount of matter in the model is thus not a free parameter: it is set by the same phase $\mu_0$ that sets the shape of the late-time curve.

**The law of internal time.** Comparing (27) with (24) gives the acceleration of internal time in the compressed phase ($\kappa\gt\kappa_h$) without a single fitted number:

$$
\Sigma^2=\frac{G(\kappa)}{G(\kappa_h)}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-\Omega_r)\,\big[1+\Omega_r(1+z)/A_m\big]}. \tag{29}
$$

The first factor is the pure geometry of the core: at strong compression $G\approx\kappa$, hence $\Sigma$ grows as $\sqrt\kappa$. The second is real light. In the most compressed phase the acceleration levels off at a constant value

$$
\Sigma_\infty=\frac{1}{K\,b_0^2\,\sqrt{\Omega_r}}\approx24.6\qquad(\mu_0=0.76). \tag{30}
$$

Below the switch-on point $\Sigma=1$ up to the correction for light, about 0.04 %. The number $\Sigma_\infty$ compares the rate that the core alone would give with the actual one. The observed rate itself is standard: the early history comes out in the form that nucleosynthesis and the CMB require, a mixture of matter and light, and at nucleosynthesis the rate coincides with that of such a mixture (Table 4). *(follows from P6)* The dust-like behaviour of compressed objectness rests on the picture of §2.1 and is not derived from the circle (§14).

Therefore standard nucleosynthesis and the model’s own sound horizon arise in DSIC as consequences of formula (27), not as a separate assumption. The measured quantities taken from outside in the process are listed in §9.5. What the model contributes is the amplitude: with $H_0=67.9$ from the CMB angle one gets $A_mh^2=0.150$ (Planck within ΛCDM gives 0.143 [14]). ### 9.4 Three phases of the early Universe

In the most compressed phase the expansion rate is set by light: under compression its density grows faster than that of objectness. The expansion proceeds as for radiation, $b\propto\sqrt\tau$. The core alone, near the zero of space, would give the same law of growth, but it would attribute it to objectness itself and with an amplitude hundreds of times larger; P6 leaves it only to real light with its measured density.

Around $z\approx3576$ the density of matter catches up with that of light, and the expansion begins to turn “dust-like”. Light is still locked in the stuck-together medium, the monolith; physically this is the photon–baryon plasma. Sound waves run through it at the speed $c_s=c/\sqrt{3(1+R_b)}$, where $R_b=3\omega_b/(4\omega_\gamma(1+z))$; by recombination this is 0.45$c$. At $z=1090$ light decouples from matter, and the CMB is born.

After that matter moves freely: the expansion is almost purely “dust-like”, $b\propto\tau^{2/3}$, and structures grow. From the switch-on point the core begins to add space, and after $z_t$ the expansion accelerates.

## 10. Joint tests

### 10.1 Supernovae, BAO and the CMB angle

The early part gives the model its own sound horizon, so it can be tested the way DESI tests dark-energy models: supernovae, BAO and the CMB angle together, without a free $r_d$. The supernovae are taken without the SH0ES calibrators (1580 SN), with analytic marginalisation over $M$, so that the Hubble tension does not decide the outcome. Each model has two parameters: $(\mu_0,H_0)$ and $(\Omega_m,H_0)$.

The result: $\Delta\chi^2=+5.4$ in favour of ΛCDM, about 2.3σ. *(against the model)* DSIC describes the supernovae slightly better and BAO noticeably worse. The reason is that the computed sound horizon is 1–2 % smaller than BAO require: at $\mu_0=0.76$ it is 145.3 against 147.6 Mpc. In other words, for DSIC the $H_0$ from the CMB angle (67.9) and the $H_0$ from BAO with its own $r_d$ (70.1) disagree. Without the BGS point at $z=0.295$, that is, on twelve points out of thirteen, the gap would be +4.4. The paper uses the full DESI DR2 set.

**Free $w_c$.** The dust assumption can be relaxed: in the rule $w_{\rm obj}=\min(w_{\rm core},0)$ the zero is replaced by a free parameter $w_c$:

$$
F(z)=F(z_c)\left(\frac{1+z}{1+z_c}\right)^{3w_c},\quad z\gt z_c;\qquad w_{\rm core}(z_c)=w_c. \tag{33}
$$

The joint fit gives $w_c=-0.0132\pm0.0032$: this is better than zero by $\Delta\chi^2=12.0$ (3.5σ) and gives $\Delta\chi^2=-6.6$ relative to ΛCDM with one extra parameter. But this is not a measurement of pressure. The parameter $w_c$ changes only the early Universe, namely the amount of matter around recombination, and through it the sound horizon: for $w_c\lt0$ there is less matter, the expansion was then slower, and sound had time to travel farther. The CMB angle holds the ratio of the sound horizon to the distance to the CMB, so $H_0$ shifts together with $r_d$. The fit chooses $w_c\lt0$ in order to lengthen the sound horizon that BAO find too short (§10.2).

The price of this solution: $H_0=62.4\pm1.4$, 6.2σ below SH0ES, matter at recombination $\omega_m=0.102$ instead of 0.146, and matter–radiation equality shifted from $z=3490$ to 2363. The full CMB spectrum measures exactly these quantities, and in the fit to it DSIC chose $\omega_m=0.142$ (Table 7). The variant with a free $w_c$ would therefore almost certainly fail the full spectrum; no CAMB calculation was done for it. Conclusion: the preference for $w_c\lt0$ is the same shortfall of the sound horizon in another guise; it says nothing about the pressure of compressed objectness. *(open)*

### 10.2 Where the sound horizon falls short

The late-time data, supernovae and the shape of BAO, prefer the phase $\mu_0=0.7617$. At this phase and with $H_0=67.61$ from the CMB angle, the model’s own sound horizon is 145.05 Mpc, while BAO need 147.75: the model is 1.83 % short. This one number accounts for the loss in the joint fits, for the disagreement between the phases preferred by supernovae and by the CMB (§10.3), and for the preference for $w_c\lt0$. *(against the model)*

Where the discrepancy sits can be seen from when the sound horizon accumulates and where the light travels. Before $z=10^6$ only 0.19 % of the sound horizon at recombination accumulates, and before $z=10^5$ only 1.86 %; moreover, the rate at nucleosynthesis is standard (Table 4). Therefore peculiarities near the very zero of space, a different flow of time or a different motion of light, cannot shift the CMB angle by per cents. If light and sound had nothing to move through there, the horizon would become shorter, and the agreement would get worse. Of the path of light to the CMB, 58 % falls between $z=2.33$ and recombination, and it is there that the model departs from the data: the ratio $D_M(z_*)/D_M(2.33)$ is 2.380 for DSIC, 2.414–2.422 is needed, and ΛCDM has 2.425. Over this stretch the expansion is driven by matter, and the model takes the amount of matter from the binding mass at the switch-on point (28).

**A probe: less matter.** Let only a fraction $f$ of the binding mass of the core at the switch-on point count as matter, while the remainder continues into the past as a constant density; the rate stays continuous, and $f=1$ is the model of the paper. The joint fit gives $f=0.922\pm0.023$, $\mu_0=0.7626\pm0.0034$, $H_0=67.44\pm0.53$ and $r_d=147.9$ Mpc (Table 6). Relative to ΛCDM this is $\Delta\chi^2=-6.7$ with one extra parameter (−4.7 after correcting for it), and $f=1$ is worse by $\Delta\chi^2=12.1$. The gain is the same as with a free $w_c$, but with an ordinary $H_0$ and with $\omega_m=0.139$ at recombination, close to what the full CMB spectrum prefers. The remainder makes up 7.8 % of the rate at the switch-on point and vanishes by recombination.

The data thus point not to pressure but to the amount of matter: the rule that sets it through the switch-on point gives more than is needed, by 7.8 % from the CMB angle and by 5.6 % from the full spectrum (§10.3). The model does not derive the value of $f$. *(hypothesis)*

**Why not move the switch-on point.** The switch-on point was not fitted to the data: it is the only point where the core decelerates like dust ($q=\tfrac12$), and the minimum of the binding mass of the core (26). Because it is a minimum, any shift of it gives more matter, not less: switching on at $z=2$ would give 0.4 % more, at $z=3$ 3.0 % more, and at $z=1.2$ and $z=4$ 12.1 and 12.3 % more. The data need 5.6 % less than the minimum itself. So the question is not where the dust phase switches on but why not all of the binding mass at the switch-on point becomes matter.

### 10.3 The full CMB spectrum

The angle $\theta_*$ is a single number. The CMB itself provides hundreds: the temperature and polarisation spectra, which show the peaks of the sound waves, their heights and their damping. To test DSIC on them, the background of the model is translated into the language of the standard code CAMB [19]. Matter, objectness with a centre, enters as ordinary matter: $\omega_c=A_mh^2-\omega_b$. The space added by the core, $X(z)$ from (31), is given to the code as a smooth “fluid” with a table $w(a)$ [20]. This is a translation, not a new substance: the form of $X(z)$ is entirely set by the core. CAMB reproduces the rate (27) to better than $10^{-4}$ all the way from today to $z=10^6$; for the $f$ probe the accuracy is $4\cdot10^{-4}$, because the jump of $w$ at the switch-on point is replaced by a smooth step of width 0.02 in $\ln(1+z)$. Recombination, sound waves, the growth of perturbations and lensing are computed by the code itself, with ordinary local physics.

The data are the Planck 2018 TT, TE and EE spectra at $\ell=30$–$2508$ in the compressed plik-lite form [21] and two TT bins at $\ell\lt30$ [22], 615 points in all. The optical depth is fixed, $\tau=0.0544$; the overall amplitude of the spectrum is fitted; neutrinos are massless. For ΛCDM the free parameters are $\omega_b$, $\omega_c$, $\theta_*$, $n_s$; for DSIC $\mu_0$, $\theta_*$, $\omega_b$, $n_s$. DSIC has no free $\omega_c$: the amount of matter is set by the core. In the $f$ probe the matter fraction $f$ is added to the DSIC parameters (§10.2); it was fitted only together with supernovae and BAO.

The result is twofold. On the CMB alone DSIC describes the whole spectrum no worse than ΛCDM: $\Delta\chi^2=-2.5$ with the same number of parameters. *(tested)* This is the strictest exam in cosmology, and the form of the early history (27) stands on it: the peaks agree because before the switch-on point the model is the same mixture of matter and light that obeys laboratory physics. The CMB also chooses the phase of the model by itself, $\mu_0=0.744\pm0.003$, and $H_0=70.5\pm0.5$, between the Planck value in ΛCDM and SH0ES.

Together with supernovae and BAO the model loses: $\Delta\chi^2=+8.8$, about 3.0σ. *(against the model)* The contributions by part: CMB $+2.8$, supernovae $-2.7$, BAO $+8.7$. The loss comes from BAO, mainly from two DESI points: at $z=0.51$ the transverse distance is 2.7σ shorter than measured, and at $z=2.33$ the radial one is 2.0σ longer. The reason is that the CMB rigidly fixes the amount of matter, which in DSIC uniquely sets the phase $\mu_0$; at that phase the distance curve cannot hit both points at once (§10.2). Supernovae alone give $\mu_0=0.760\pm0.009$, supernovae together with BAO and a free $r_d$ give $0.7617\pm0.0029$, and the CMB $0.7444\pm0.0028$. Within the model this is a disagreement of phases: about 1.7σ between supernovae and the CMB, and about 4.3σ between the late-time data as a whole and the CMB.

**The $f$ probe with the full spectrum.** The same joint fit, but with a free matter fraction, gives $f=0.944\pm0.014$ (the last column of Table 7). The value $f=1$ is worse by $\Delta\chi^2=16.5$, about 4.1σ. The CMB angle alone gave $0.922\pm0.023$; this agrees with the full spectrum (a difference of 0.8σ). Relative to ΛCDM $\Delta\chi^2=-7.7$, and there is a gain in all three parts: CMB $-1.6$, supernovae $-3.7$, BAO $-2.4$. Allowing for the extra parameter, the Akaike criterion leaves $-5.7$, while the Bayesian criterion (a penalty of $\ln N$ for $N=2208$ points) gives $+0.05$, that is, a tie. Matter at recombination is $\omega_m=0.142$, almost the same as in ΛCDM (0.141); $H_0=67.9\pm0.4$, $r_d=147.2$ Mpc. The atoms and the spectral tilt stay ordinary: $\omega_b=0.02242$, $n_s=0.9670$.

The phase $\mu_0=0.7604\pm0.0027$ coincides with the phase from supernovae alone ($0.7604$) and differs from the phase from supernovae with BAO by 0.3σ. The disagreement of phases, which is 4.3σ for $f=1$, disappears with this matter fraction. Hence the three weak spots of the model, the loss on BAO, the shortfall of the sound horizon and the two phases, come down to one rule: the amount of matter set by the switch-on point (28). The model does not derive the value of $f$; until it follows from the principles, it is a fitted parameter. The probe does not remove the Hubble tension: 4.6σ from SH0ES, against 4.1σ for ΛCDM in the same fit. *(hypothesis)*

The assumptions of the calculation are the same for all models: the compressed Planck likelihood instead of the full one, a fixed $\tau$, zero neutrino masses, a smooth addition of space $X$ with no perturbations of its own, and growth of matter perturbations according to general relativity. This is a fair approximation, but not a full Planck analysis.

## 11. The language of dark energy

To compare DSIC with what DESI sees, it can be translated into the language of “matter + dark energy”. DSIC has no dark energy as a substance; this is purely a translation. The matter in it is the dust amplitude $A_m$ (28), so there is no arbitrariness, and the “dark energy” is the space $X(z)$ added by the core (31). Its effective equation of state is

$$
w_X(z)=-1+\frac13\,\frac{d\ln X}{d\ln(1+z)}. \tag{34}
$$

The result is a characteristic picture (numbers at $\mu_0=0.76$). Today $w_X=-0.92$. Around $z=0.27$ it crosses $-1$, becomes “phantom” deeper in the past, and the density of $X$ drops to zero at the switch-on point: $X(2)/X(0)=0.064$. A fit of the form $w_0+w_a(1-a)$ over $z\le1$ gives $w_0\approx-0.78$, $w_a\approx-1.2$. DESI DR2 together with the CMB and supernovae finds the same direction: $w_0=-0.838\pm0.055$, $w_a=-0.62^{+0.22}_{-0.19}$ with Pantheon+; $-0.667\pm0.088$, $-1.09^{+0.31}_{-0.27}$ with Union3; $-0.752\pm0.057$, $-0.86^{+0.23}_{-0.20}$ with DESY5 [3].

This agreement must be treated with caution. The $(w_0,w_a)$ fit depends strongly on the chosen interval (over $z\le1.5$ it gives $w_0=-0.49$, $w_a=-2.4$), because the true DSIC curve is not linear in $a$. The agreement in direction is qualitative. Only the joint fit of §10 tests it quantitatively, and there DSIC still loses. *(open)*

## 12. Time at different places and early objects

Only the core has a common time. Inside space time is relative: every kind of matter has its own. In the homogeneous average the clocks of all comoving observers run alike, but this is not a common time; it is a consequence of identical conditions. Places with a different history of compression and different connectedness (§2.1) live by different clocks.

**A strict test.** The light curves of distant supernovae are stretched in time as $(1+z)^n$. From 1504 DES supernovae at $0.1\le z\le1.2$, $n=1.003\pm0.005\pm0.010$ has been measured (statistical and systematic errors) [23]. So in the late Universe the time of distant objects differs from ours by exactly the factor $1+z$, with no additions at the per-cent level. DSIC satisfies this automatically: the period of a light wave is stretched in the same way as its length. *(tested)*

**Black holes.** With the law of internal time, the time budget of early objects is almost the same as in ΛCDM. For six known supermassive black holes at $z=7$–$11$ [24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29], growth at the Eddington limit (45 Myr per $e$-folding) from a light seed of 100 solar masses has enough time only for 1 of 6, both in DSIC and in ΛCDM (1 of 6). From seeds of $10^4$ solar masses all six make it in both models. The shortage of time is a common problem of cosmology, not a feature of DSIC.

**A hypothesis: unbroken remnants.** A different picture is natural in DSIC. The most compressed parts of the monolith may not have broken up when the medium around them was freed. What these clumps are is unknown; most likely they are something like black holes. Then a massive black hole is not an object assembled quickly but a remnant whose mass is inherited. This is close to the heavy and primordial seeds now seriously discussed. JWST finds what such a picture expects: a “dormant” black hole at $z=6.68$ with a mass of about $4\cdot10^8\,M_\odot$, about 40 % of the mass of its whole galaxy, accreting at about 2 % of the limit [30]; and the object Abell 2744-QSO1 at $z\approx7$, whose black-hole mass estimate exceeds the estimate of its stellar mass [31]. The authors of the first work explain it by bursts of super-Eddington accretion, so this is one of several explanations, not a proof. *(hypothesis)*

**The limit of the hypothesis.** If a clump contained stars living by the accelerated time of their epoch, then, “frozen” at the compression of recombination, by $z=7.3$ they would have about 5 Gyr of proper time against 0.7 for the background. No such old stars are seen. There is no contradiction if the clump is dark, like a black hole: its internal time is not observable from outside, and the stars around it are born later from the surrounding medium and live by background time. The hypothesis then expects very massive black holes in galaxies with young stars. The test is the massive “quenched” galaxies at $z=7.3$ [32]: their stars must be no older than the background.

## 13. How to refute the model

A model is useful only if it can be refuted. Below are the tests that appear decisive, and the criteria.

- **The joint fit of supernovae, BAO and the CMB.** At present $\Delta\chi^2=+5.4$ against DSIC with the CMB angle alone and $+8.8$ with the full spectrum; the second number is already close to the threshold. If with DESI DR3 and new supernova catalogues the gap exceeds 10 under the same assumptions, the model in its present form can be considered rejected. If with DESI DR3 the shortfall of the sound horizon (§10.2) persists, the rule that sets the amount of matter through the switch-on point will have to change. The $f$ probe gives such a rule a numerical target: $f=0.944\pm0.014$. A rule derived from the principles must hit it in advance, not by fitting; if new data move $f$ toward 1, the probe loses its point.
- **The density of dark energy at $z\approx1.5$–$2.5$.** DSIC requires the effective dark energy to fall almost to zero by the switch-on point: $X(2)/X(0)=0.064$ at $\mu_0=0.76$. A reconstruction showing a constant density at these $z$ refutes the model.
- **The shape of $Om(z)$ at $z\lt2$.** DSIC: 0.355 at $z=0.2$ and 0.301 at $z=2$. A flat $Om(z)$ measured with an independently fixed $H_0$ works against DSIC.
- **The onset of acceleration.** DSIC gives $z_t$ from 0.766 to 0.894 for the phases preferred by supernovae and by the CMB; ΛCDM with the Planck parameters gives 0.63. A model-independent measurement with an accuracy of ±0.03 will distinguish the models and at the same time show which of the DSIC phases is compatible with it.
- **Time dilation.** Any reliable deviation of the exponent $n$ from 1 for supernovae contradicts the principle on which the redshift is built.

Radial BAO at $z=3$–$3.5$ will not distinguish the models: the phase of free matter acts there, and the difference in $D_H/r_d$ between DSIC and ΛCDM is from 0.7 % at $z=3$ to 0.2 % at $z=3.5$ (for the joint fits of Table 6).

## 14. Limitations and open questions

- **Why compressed objectness is dust.** *(open)* P6 itself follows from space being a common container with no outer side. But that compressed objectness behaves as dust rests on the picture of §2.1: the size of an object is held by its own centre, while the length of a light wave is set by space. This is not derived from the circle. The limiting acceleration $\Sigma_\infty$ is set by the real density of light: it is measured, but the model does not predict it.
- **The shortfall of the sound horizon.** *(against the model)* At the phase of the late-time data the model’s sound horizon is 1.8 % shorter than BAO require; hence the loss in the joint fits and the disagreement of phases. The discrepancy sits between $z\approx2$ and recombination, where matter drives the expansion, and not near the zero of space (§10.2). A free $w_c$ closes it at the cost of $H_0\approx62$. A matter fraction $f=0.944\pm0.014$ closes it with the full CMB spectrum as well, at an ordinary $H_0=67.9$ (§10.3). But $f$ is not derived: one needs to understand why not all of the binding mass at the switch-on point becomes matter, but only about 0.94 of it. Moving the switch-on point itself does not help: it only adds matter (§10.2). We note a coincidence: $f$ differs from $\mu_h=0.950$, the phase at the switch-on point (16), by 0.5σ, and from $\mu_h^2$ by 2.9σ. A factor $\mu_h$ could come from internal time (7), but there is no derivation, and without one, a single coincidence among many possible combinations proves nothing.
- **The measurement rule $b=(P/Q)/\mu$.** *(open)* The data support the factor $1/\mu$, but it does not follow from the postulates: a measurement with the rod or with the clock alone gives one factor, not two (§4).
- **The clocks of compressed matter and P5.** *(open)* The derivation of the clock rate (7) assumes a constant speed of light at core scale, while the law (24) drops this assumption. This does not affect the numbers, but there is as yet no single derivation of both rules from one principle.
- **Non-luminous matter.** *(open)* The model sets the amount of matter itself, $A_mh^2\approx0.15$, and this matter drives the growth of structure. Atoms make up about 15 % of it; what the other 85 % is, the model does not say.
- **Perturbations and the CMB spectrum.** *(open)* The full spectrum was computed with a standard code under the assumption that matter grows according to general relativity and that the addition of space is smooth. DSIC has no perturbation theory of its own; the full Planck likelihood (low-$\ell$ polarisation, lensing) was not used. The spectral tilt $n_s$ is fitted, not derived.
- **The uniformity of the CMB.** *(open)* Formula (27) gives an ordinary finite particle horizon in the early Universe, so the question of why the CMB is the same in causally disconnected regions of the sky stands as it does in the standard model without inflation. Whether the previous phase of the cycle can resolve it has not been studied.
- **Two phases.** *(against the model)* Supernovae and the CMB prefer different $\mu_0$, and $z_t$, $z_h$, $H_0$ and $A_m$ shift together with the phase (Table 5). This is a consequence of the same shortfall of the sound horizon: with a matter fraction $f\approx0.94$ the phases agree (§10.3).
- **The Hubble tension.** *(open)* From the full CMB spectrum DSIC gives $H_0=70.5\pm0.5$, closer to SH0ES than ΛCDM, but together with BAO and supernovae 69.2, and in the $f$ probe 67.9, 4.6σ below SH0ES. The tension is not removed.
- **The local picture.** *(open)* The common centre of fall of bound systems and the arrival of space in the voids are described only qualitatively. What is needed is a measure of connectedness at which a centre becomes common, and a test of whether such inhomogeneous expansion differs from the homogeneous average in the data.
- **The zero of space.** *(open)* The motion of the core at the zero of space is singular (4), and the state of matter there is unknown. What “eversion” means in the language of local physics, the model does not say.
- **Clumps.** *(open)* What they are is unknown. The time rule for a single clump is yet to be found.

## 15. Conclusion

The starting thought is one: there is no absolute scale, and space and objectness are two poles of one norm. If the objectness fraction flows uniformly and the observer is made of objectness, its rod and clock give one function, $b(\mu)=\sqrt{1-\mu^2}/\mu^2$. The whole late Universe follows from it: $b^2=e^{4k\tau}-e^{2k\tau}$, the onset of acceleration at $\mu^2=3-\sqrt5$, a finite horizon. On supernovae and BAO this geometry with one shape parameter describes the data on a par with flat ΛCDM. In this picture acceleration is not the action of a hidden component but the divergence of two scale factors, seen from inside.

The core alone does not explain the early Universe, and it is shown why: light does not have time to arrive from the CMB, and nucleosynthesis runs too fast. The second principle, that matter does not go beyond space, turns this failure into a programme. The pressure of space compresses the medium, not the size of objects in the observer’s units (their own falling into themselves does not change because of it); in compressed matter internal time runs faster. The point where this switches on follows from the core itself. The law of internal time follows with no fitted numbers, and the model computes the sound horizon by itself, obtains standard nucleosynthesis, and describes the full CMB spectrum no worse than ΛCDM.

At the same time the model is not better than ΛCDM. Together with supernovae and BAO it loses: by $\Delta\chi^2=+5.4$ with the CMB angle alone and by $+8.8$ with the full spectrum. The model’s sound horizon is 1.8 % shorter than BAO require, and this is also the source of the disagreement between the phases preferred by supernovae and by the CMB. The full CMB spectrum together with supernovae and BAO shows what is behind it: if there is 5.6 % less matter than the switch-on point gives ($f=0.944\pm0.014$), the model describes all three data sets no worse than ΛCDM ($\Delta\chi^2=-7.7$; allowing for the extra parameter, a tie by the Bayesian criterion), and the phases agree. Deriving this fraction from the principles is the main task of the next step. The dust-like behaviour of compressed matter rests on a qualitative picture, and the growth of structure needs non-luminous matter: the model sets its amount but not its nature. The value of the model lies elsewhere: in the origin of the curve. The same expansion history follows from one geometric starting point, and the effective dark energy changes in the same direction that DESI points to. The data of the coming years will decide.

## Appendix A. Formula map

The appendix consists of two lists. The first shows how the formulas are applied: what to put in at the input and in what order to compute in order to obtain an observable quantity. The second shows how the formulas are derived from one another and what status each has.

### A.1 How to apply the formulas

The steps go in order. The input is three numbers: the phase $\mu_0$, the Hubble constant $H_0$ and the redshift $z$. The output is the quantities compared with data. The only branching is the check of whether compression is switched on.

1. **Phase today** — $\mu_0$. The only parameter of the core: the objectness fraction today. Supernovae give about 0.760, the full CMB spectrum about 0.744.
2. **Redshift** — $z$. The epoch for which the answer is needed. It sets how many times smaller the observable scale was than today.
3. **Hubble constant** — $H_0$. The expansion rate today, km/s/Mpc. It can be set by hand or found from the condition that the CMB angle equals the measured one.
4. **Scale today** — $b_0=\dfrac{\sqrt{1-\mu_0^2}}{\mu_0^2}$ (8). The observable scale: space in the observer’s rods, converted by the observer’s clock. Uses the result of step 1.
5. **Scale at epoch z** — $B=\dfrac{b_0}{1+z}$ (9). Redshift is a ratio of scales: today’s to that at the moment of emission. Uses the results of steps 2, 4.
6. **Core rate today** — $K=\dfrac{2-\mu_0^2}{1-\mu_0^2},\quad k=\dfrac{H_0}{K}$ (14). The number K links today’s observed rate with the core constant k. Uses the results of steps 1, 3.
7. **Phase at epoch z** — $s=\sqrt{1+4B^2},\quad \mu^2=\dfrac{2}{1+s}$ (10). The scale formula inverted: from the scale of an epoch one finds the objectness fraction in it. The expression stays stable even at the very zero of space. Uses the result of step 5.
8. **Degree of compression** — $\kappa=\dfrac{\mu}{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}$ (23). The ratio of the moduli of the poles, Q/P: how many times more objectness there is than space. Uses the result of step 7.
9. **Rate of the core alone** — $E_{\rm core}=\dfrac{1}{K}\cdot\dfrac{2-\mu^2}{1-\mu^2}$ (12). The expansion rate given by the core without compression, in units of today’s rate. Uses the results of steps 6, 7.
10. **Compression on?** — $\kappa>\kappa_h,\quad \kappa_h^2=4+2\sqrt7$ (26). The threshold is not fitted: it is the minimum of the binding mass of the core, which is also the point q = ½. Before this epoch matter is compressed. Uses the result of step 8.
11. **No: the core as it is** — $\varphi=1$ (§5). The late Universe: the formulas of the core apply unchanged, and the distance is taken in closed form. Uses the result of step 10 (answer “no”).
12. **Yes: binding mass locked** — $\varphi=\dfrac{G(\kappa_h)}{G(\kappa)}$ (25). The early Universe: locked objectness is not lost and behaves as dust. The function G(κ) = (2 + κ²)²(1 + κ²)^{3/2}/κ⁶. Uses the result of step 10 (answer “yes”).
13. **Expansion rate** — $E^2=(1-\Omega_r)\,\varphi\,E_{\rm core}^2+\Omega_r(1+z)^4$ (27). One formula for the whole history: matter with the amplitude set by the core, plus real light. The observed rate is H = H₀E. This is the rate formula of the main text, written through the factor φ. Uses the results of steps 9, 11, 12.
14. **Internal time** — $\Sigma=\dfrac{E_{\rm core}}{E}$ (24). How many times faster time runs in compressed matter. Below the switch-on point Σ = 1 up to the contribution of light. Uses the results of steps 9, 13.
15. **Radial distance** — $D_H=\dfrac{c}{H_0E}$ (§6). The distance along the line of sight per unit redshift. Uses the result of step 13.
16. **Age at epoch z** — $t=\displaystyle\int_z^\infty\dfrac{dz'}{(1+z')\,H}$ (§9.5). The observer’s proper time from the zero of space to epoch z. Uses the result of step 13.
17. **Chronometers** (§7). 33 measurements of H(z) from the ages of stars in galaxies. The rate itself, H = c/D_H, is compared. Uses the result of step 15.
18. **Transverse distance** — $D_M=\displaystyle\int_0^z D_H\,dz'$ (18). The comoving distance. In the late Universe the integral is taken in closed form. Uses the result of step 15.
19. **Sound horizon** — $r_s=\displaystyle\int_z^\infty\dfrac{c_s}{c}\,D_H\,dz'$ (32). The path of sound in the plasma up to epoch z: up to recombination (r*) and up to the end of baryon drag (r_d). Uses the result of step 15.
20. **Supernova brightness** — $d_L=(1+z)\,D_M,\quad m-M=5\lg d_L+25$ (§6). The luminosity distance and the distance modulus, with d_L in megaparsecs. Uses the result of step 18.
21. **BAO, DESI DR2** — $D_M/r_d,\quad D_H/r_d$ (§7). 13 measurements of distances in units of the sound horizon r_d. Uses the results of steps 15, 18, 19.
22. **CMB angle** — $\theta_*=\dfrac{r_s(z_*)}{D_M(z_*)}$ (§9.5). The angular size of the sound horizon at recombination. Equating it to the measured value gives H₀. Uses the results of steps 18, 19.
23. **Supernovae, Pantheon+** (§7). 1657 supernovae: the distance modulus is compared. Uses the result of step 20.
24. **CMB, Planck 2018** (§10.3). The CMB angle and the full spectrum (via CAMB). Uses the result of step 22.

### A.2 How the formulas are derived

Each item is one step of the model: a formula, its meaning in one phrase, its status, and what it follows from. The late Universe comes first, then the early one. A “postulate” is adopted without derivation, “derived” follows from the postulates mathematically, “tested” is confirmed by calculation on data, and “against the model” is a result that does not favour DSIC.

**The late Universe: the core**

- **Norm of the poles** *(postulate P2)* — $S^2+O^2=R^2$. The scale factors of space and objectness are tied by one norm: one grows only at the expense of the other. See §2.
- **Uniform flow** *(postulate P3)* — $|d\mu/dt|=k,\quad \mu=|O|/R$. In core time the objectness fraction changes uniformly. This time cannot be observed from inside. See §2. Follows from: norm of the poles.
- **The observer’s projection** *(postulate P4)* — $P=R\sqrt{1-\mu^2},\quad Q=R\mu$. An observer inside sees only the moduli of the poles and their ratios. See §4. Follows from: norm of the poles.
- **Rod and clock** *(derived from P4, P5)* — $L\propto\mu,\quad d\tau=dt/\mu$. The rod is made of objectness; the constancy of the speed of light selects a unique clock rate. See §4. Follows from: the observer’s projection.
- **Observable scale** *(measurement rule)* — $b(\mu)=\dfrac{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}{\mu^2},\quad 1+z=\dfrac{b_0}{b}$. Space in rods, converted by the clock. The clock factor is adopted as a postulate; the data support it. See §4. Follows from: the observer’s projection, rod and clock.
- **Expansion history** *(derived)* — $\mu=e^{-k\tau},\quad b^2=e^{4k\tau}-e^{2k\tau}$. Growth as √τ at first, exponential at the end. By the observer’s clock the zero of objectness is unreachable. See §5. Follows from: uniform flow, rod and clock, observable scale.
- **Rate and deceleration** *(derived)* — $H=k\,\dfrac{2-\mu^2}{1-\mu^2},\quad q=\dfrac{-\mu^4+6\mu^2-4}{(2-\mu^2)^2}$. Acceleration begins at μ² = 3 − √5; the point q = ½ is at μ² = (8 − 2√7)/3. See §5. Follows from: expansion history.
- **Distances and horizon** *(derived)* — $\chi=\dfrac{c}{k}\,b_0\big[\sqrt{1-\mu_0^2}-\sqrt{1-\mu_e^2}\,\big]$. Distance in closed form; the particle horizon of the core is finite. See §6. Follows from: expansion history.
- **Late-Universe data** *(tested)*. Supernovae: Δχ² = −0.1. Supernovae and BAO with a free $r_{d}$: −5.0. Chronometers and the ratio $D_{M}$/$D_{H}$ do not distinguish the models. See §7. Follows from: rate and deceleration, distances and horizon.

**The early Universe: compression**

- **Limit of the core** *(derived)* — $\dfrac{D_M(z_*)}{D_M(2.33)}\lt 1.89\lt 2.42$. The core gives a distance ratio of at most 1.89, while the data require 2.42: the CMB lies beyond its horizon. The rate at nucleosynthesis is 24 times too high. See §8. Follows from: distances and horizon, rate and deceleration.
- **Compression** *(postulate P6)* — $\kappa=\dfrac{Q}{P},\quad w_{\rm obj}=\min(w_{\rm core},0)$. Matter is contained in space and cannot leave it: when the scale of space is small, matter is compressed as in a press. Compressed objectness behaves as dust. See §9. Follows from: the observer’s projection, limit of the core.
- **Internal time** *(postulate P6)* — $d\tau=\Sigma(\kappa)\,dt/\mu\iff H=H_{\rm core}/\Sigma$. In compressed matter time runs Σ times faster. See §9.3. Follows from: compression, rod and clock.
- **Binding mass and the switch-on point** *(derived)* — $G(\kappa)=\dfrac{(2+\kappa^2)^2(1+\kappa^2)^{3/2}}{\kappa^6},\quad \kappa_h^2=4+2\sqrt7$. The minimum of the binding mass of the core coincides with the point q = ½. The threshold is not fitted. See §9.3. Follows from: compression, rate and deceleration, observable scale.
- **One rate formula** *(follows from P6)* — $E^2=(1-\Omega_r)(1+z)^3F(\min(z,z_h))+\Omega_r(1+z)^4$. Matter with the amplitude set by the core, real light, and the space added by the core. See §9.3. Follows from: binding mass and the switch-on point, internal time.
- **The law of Σ** *(follows from P6)* — $\Sigma^2=\dfrac{G(\kappa)}{G(\kappa_h)}\cdot\dfrac{1}{(1-\Omega_r)\,[1+\Omega_r(1+z)/A_m]}$. Growth as √κ and saturation near 24.6, set by the measured density of light. See §9.3. Follows from: one rate formula, internal time.
- **Sound horizon and H₀** *(computed)* — $r_s=\displaystyle\int_z^\infty\frac{c_s\,dz'}{H},\quad \theta_*=\frac{r_s(z_*)}{D_M(z_*)}$. At μ₀ = 0.76: r* = 142.7 Mpc, H₀ = 67.9, age 13.69 Gyr. See §9.5. Follows from: one rate formula.
- **The language of dark energy** *(derived)* — $w_X=-1+\dfrac13\dfrac{d\ln X}{d\ln(1+z)}$. Today w = −0.92, it crosses −1 at z = 0.27, and the density vanishes at the switch-on point. See §11. Follows from: one rate formula.
- **Joint test** *(against the model)*. Supernovae, 13 BAO points and the CMB angle with the model’s own $r_{d}$: Δχ² = +5.4 in favour of ΛCDM. The sound horizon is 1.8 % shorter than BAO require; the data point to about 6 % less matter. See §10.1. Follows from: sound horizon and h₀, late-universe data.
- **Full CMB spectrum** *(tested; against the model)*. CMB alone: Δχ² = −2.5, on a par with ΛCDM. Together with supernovae and BAO: +8.8; with the matter fraction f = 0.944: −7.7. See §10.3. Follows from: one rate formula, joint test.

## Appendix C. Data, method and reproduction

All numbers in the tables of the paper are produced by a single script, `dsic_test.py`, from the repository [github.com/az-zase/DSIC](https://github.com/az-zase/DSIC); the full output of the Google Colab runs from which the numbers of the paper are taken is in the same place, in the file `dsic_test_results.txt`. numpy and scipy are required; for the full CMB spectrum the script installs CAMB itself and downloads the open compressed Planck likelihood.

- `python dsic_test.py`: the core, the early Universe, the language of dark energy, the tables of the late and joint fits, the analysis of the sound-horizon shortfall, structure growth, and the time budget of black holes. About a minute, including the data download.
- `python dsic_test.py --cmb`: in addition, five fits with the full CMB spectrum, the four of Table 7 and the $f$ probe. About two hours.
- `python dsic_test.py --cmb-f`: of the full-spectrum fits, only the $f$ probe. From 20 minutes to an hour.

**Data.** The script downloads them from the official sources and checks them against copies embedded in the file.

- Supernovae: Pantheon+SH0ES, the distance and covariance files STAT+SYS and STAT-ONLY from the collaboration’s repository [8, 9]. Selection: $z_{\rm HD}\gt0.01$ or SH0ES calibrator (1657 of 1701 rows); without calibrators, 1580.
- BAO: the DESI DR2 data vector and covariance, thirteen measurements: $D_V/r_d$ for BGS and the pairs $D_M/r_d$, $D_H/r_d$ for six tracers [3].
- Chronometers: 33 measurements of $H(z)$ at $0.07\le z\le1.965$; for fifteen of them the covariance is used following the recipe of [11].
- CMB: the angle $100\,\theta_*=1.04110\pm0.00031$ [14]; the full spectrum, plik-lite [21, 22].
- Structure growth: 18 measurements of $f\sigma_8$ [16, 17, 18].

**Conventions.** Flat geometry; massless neutrinos, $N_{\rm eff}=3.046$; $T_{\rm CMB}=2.7255$ K; $\omega_b=0.02237$ in the calculations with the CMB angle. Luminosity distance $d_L=(1+z_{\rm hel})\,\chi(z_{\rm HD})$. Parameter errors come from the $\Delta\chi^2=1$ profile or from the matrix of second derivatives.

**Independent run.** The whole calculation, including the four fits with the full CMB spectrum, was repeated on a different machine (Google Colab, 16 November 2026). The $f$ probe with the full spectrum was computed there on 17 November 2026 (442 CAMB calls, 34 minutes). The numbers of the paper are taken from these runs. Compared with the calculation on the machine where the paper was built, all other numbers agree in every digit shown; the $f$ probe with the full spectrum was not run to completion there.

**A check on paper.** Take $\mu_0=0.76$. Then $b_0=0.6499/0.5776=1.1252$ and $K=(2-0.5776)/(1-0.5776)=3.367$. At $\mu=\sqrt{3-\sqrt5}=0.8740$ one gets $b=0.4859/0.7639=0.6360$ and $z_t=b_0/b-1=0.769$.
